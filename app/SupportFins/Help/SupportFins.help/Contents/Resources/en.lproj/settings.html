Support Fins Help

Settings

Each setting's tooltip, in the order of the inspector. Settings that do not apply in the current mode are hidden; search the inspector (⌥⌘F) to find one.

Printer

Your printer: nozzle, layer height, support gap, interface material.

Nozzle
Your printer's nozzle diameter. The walls, contact tips and tines are sized in nozzle lines, so they widen for a bigger nozzle and the tines narrow for a smaller one.
Layer height
Set this to the layer height you slice at. The grip tines are one layer tall so they snap off clean; if this doesn't match your slicer, the tines tear and leave marks. Default 0.2mm.
Support gap
Clearance between a support top and the part. Bigger = cleaner surface / easier removal; too big stops holding the overhang. Default 0.2mm.
Interface material
For a printer with two or more toolheads. The top layer of a wall exports as its own part of the supports, shown purple: give it a material that won't bond to the part (PETG under PLA) for a clean release. Flat contacts: only under flat undersides, where it works and costs a few tool changes. Everywhere: every wall top and the tines too, a tool change every layer on a tilted part. 3MF only.

Placement

Placement
Auto — place supports for me. Full coverage — every overhang. Draw — place them by hand.

Tines

Tines
Tines fuse the support to the part so it grips instead of just propping. Off = plain breakaway wall.
Tine grip
How tightly to space the grip tines. Light = fewest marks (default), a per-wall floor keeps grip; Firm = dense comb / max grip for a tippy or tall part. With Vertical (resin) tines this becomes Pins per wall: How many pins each wall gets. More pins = a firmer hold; fewer = fewer marks to clean up. Add pins before deepening them: a deeper pin leaves a bigger mark and is harder to snap off.
Direction
Horizontal: one-layer tines that grip the part and snap off clean (FDM). Vertical: pins that stand up from each wall into the part, like a resin slicer's contact tips, for resin printers, where every support has to be attached.
Pin depth
How far each pin sinks into the part, like a resin slicer's contact depth. Resin slicers sink a tip 0-0.2 mm; deeper leaves a bigger mark and is harder to snap off. Default 0.1 mm.

Cutouts and by-hand tools

Cutouts
Cut holes through tall breakaway walls to save filament. The top edge that holds the part, the foot, and the wall's ends stay solid, and every hole has a pointed roof so it prints without bridging. Short walls stay solid.
+ Add walls by hand
Place extra breakaway walls by hand, on top of the auto-placed ones.
Remove fins
Click a fin to remove just that one. Esc or right-click cancels.
Restore all
Bring back every fin removed in this orientation.

More settings: Hold the part

Rounded feet
Round FDM support foot corners inside the existing clearance envelope. Off keeps the original feet. Rounding reduces plate contact area; check the first layers in your slicer and print-test before using it for tall parts.
Bed pad
A tilted part rests on an edge and peels off the plate without a pad. Auto: Light, or Sure hold when the part meets the plate on a point or a small round foot (it shows which). Light: one layer, 0.12mm off the part, peels off like a brim. Sure hold: thicker and tacked into the part, holds harder but is harder to remove. Custom: set every number yourself.
Pad thickness
How tall the pad is. Only the first layer grips the plate; more layers make it stiffer and harder to peel.
Pad gap
Sideways clearance between the pad and the part's first layer. 0.12mm lets first-layer squish just close it, the way a slicer brim holds; bigger comes off easier and holds less. Under 0.1mm most slicers merge the gap and the pad welds on. 0 = no gap (Pad grip decides).
Pad grip
Where the pad meets the part's underside. Positive bites in to hold harder; 0 is flush; negative leaves a gap that snaps off cleaner.
Pad spread
How far the pad spreads past the part's contact with the plate. More spread = more plate grip.
Wall lines
How many nozzle lines thick the walls, wedges and sway braces are. 2 is the standard wall. More lines make stiffer supports for tall parts. The contact tip and tines stay one line wide, so the supports still break off the same way.

More settings: Tall parts

Brace tall walls
Walls taller than the selected height get triangular ribs at the plate, off both faces, so they don't sway as they print. Untick for plain walls.
Brace walls above
Walls taller than this height get face ribs. 5 cm or more; default 10 cm.
Sway braces
Stand tapered buttress ribs against the upright sides of a tall part, tied on with tines all the way up, so it doesn't drift or wobble as it grows. Hover an upright side to preview one, and click to add it.
Brace grip from
Height the brace tines start at. 0 = grip the whole height; raise it to tie on only above where the part starts to move.
Brace tine spacing
Vertical distance between brace tines. Smaller = held tighter, more marks on the side. Default 6mm.
Brace depth
How far the brace reaches out at the bed, as a share of its height. Deeper = stiffer, more plastic. Default 15%.
Full brace depth
Use the selected Brace depth percentage beyond the usual 60 mm limit. Larger braces need more plate space and material; collisions can shorten or refuse them. Off keeps the 60 mm limit.
Cross-rib height
Cross-rib height as a percentage of sway brace height. 10–90%, default 50%. Requires Brace tall walls and the brace to exceed its start height. Does not change the horizontal reach.

More settings: Where supports go

Wide-face coverage
How densely to line a WIDE overhang face with fins. The middle is the anti-sag default; drag LEFT for fewer fins (a small part can go down to one — the readout warns if a broad face then risks sagging), RIGHT for more support. Narrow parts are unaffected.
Plate only
Stand every support on the build plate, never on the part itself. An overhang with part under it is left unsupported, and the readout says how many.
Separate walls
Full coverage lets a wall cross another or share its base on the plate, so a crowded part peels off as one support. Tick to keep every wall apart: a resin print cures joined walls into one block that won't break away. Ticks itself for Vertical (resin) tines. Thin ties between tall walls stay: they snap. Can leave a little more overhang bare.
Long walls
Full coverage lays rows of long walls along the part's length first, the way you'd draw them by hand, then fills what they miss. Holds more of the overhang and uses more plastic. Untick for short walls under each overhang patch.
Curved walls
Let the walls under a curved overhang bend with it down to the plate wherever they can, even at more plastic. Extra walls cover what the curves miss. Auto and Full coverage only.

More settings: Display

Highlight small overhangs
Shade small overhangs amber: area-based slivers and, after generation, small-hole ceilings left for bridging. Off hides their shading; the readout still counts them.
Show midair starts
Draw a red cone under each spot whose first layer starts in midair. Off hides the cones; the readout and the card still count them.
