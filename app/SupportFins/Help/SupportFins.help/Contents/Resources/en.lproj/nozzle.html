Nozzle size and wall lines

Adapted from Per Hjort's nozzle-profile guide (#210).

Set Setup → Nozzle to your printer's nozzle diameter, and pick Walls → Wall lines for how thick the supports should be. The Walls summary shows the wall thickness.

What changes

Every support width was tuned on a 0.4 mm nozzle: a 1.0 mm wall (two 0.5 mm lines), a 0.6 mm contact tip, 0.5 mm tines. A different nozzle scales them by nozzle / 0.4:

0.4 0.6 0.8 1.0 1.6 2.0
wall, 2 lines 1.0 1.5 2.0 2.5 4.0 5.0
contact tip 0.6 0.9 1.2 1.5 2.4 3.0
tine 0.5 0.75 1.0 1.25 2.0 2.5
  • A smaller nozzle (0.2, 0.25) narrows the tip and tines, so they leave a smaller mark. Walls, wedges and braces never get thinner than the 0.4 numbers.
  • Wall lines multiplies the walls, wedges and braces: 4 lines is twice the wall, 8 is four times. The tip and tines stay one line, so the supports break off the same way.
  • Heights don't follow the nozzle. Tines are one layer tall, set by Layer height.

Limits

  • Layer height goes up to 0.6 mm. The breakaway gap, the feet and the bed pad are tuned for thin layers; thicker layers need those to scale first.
  • Walls stop at 6 mm, half the 12 mm spacing between rows of walls, so a row never fuses into one block. A big nozzle offers only the Wall lines that fit (a 2.0 nozzle: 2 lines; 1.2: up to 4), and the nozzle goes up to 2.0.
  • Thicker walls fit fewer places. A big nozzle or 8 lines can leave a tight overhang without a wall; the readout lists what's left unsupported.
  • The plugins build 0.4 / 2 lines for now (the PrusaSlicer and Onshape plugins take a nozzle).

Check in the slicer

Sizes other than 0.4 / 2 lines follow these rules but haven't been print-tested. After slicing:

  • the walls slice as solid perimeters with no gaps down the middle
  • each tine shows as one line on one layer, touching the part
  • there's a visible gap layer between each wall top and the part
  • first layers: feet and pad stick and stay separate from the part
